---
title: "Be proactive - don't wait for something to come to you"
description: "If you see something that needs doing and you can do it, do it. Waiting to be asked is a choice. Proactiveness is about having enough context to see what's coming before it arrives."
---

If you see something that needs doing and you can do it, do it. That's been true in every role I've had, especially in the first weeks: auditing what's already there, noticing what's broken or inefficient, and fixing it without waiting to be asked. It was never about being exceptional; it's just how I approach work.

Proactiveness comes down to context: having enough of it, and enough curiosity, to see what's coming before it arrives, and acting on it. Busy is easy. Staying ahead of it takes attention. I flagged a vendor's usage approaching its limit before it ran out, and proposed a workaround without anyone asking for one. The Web Team Vision document existed before anyone had commissioned it. Improvements to the company's recognition culture were on the table ahead of anyone naming the problem out loud. A conference business case was ready well before the question of attending ever came up.

Waiting to be asked is a choice. I've tried to make the other choice the default.
